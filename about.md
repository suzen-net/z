# About

I'm Ziya Suzen. I started programming at fourteen, and it became my job
in 1993: tourism, banking and internet providers in Cyprus, internet
infrastructure in Amsterdam, and more than a decade in London across
public transport, investment banking and telecoms, much of it on
distributed systems.

These days I do the engineering at [Cienvita](https://www.cienvita.com/),
a family-run software company in beautiful
[County Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Durham), in the
North East of England, that I run with my wife Rachel. The last couple
of years have gone heavily into applied AI, alongside systems work like
the projects below.

## Open source

* [nats.net](https://github.com/nats-io/nats.net), the NATS client for
  .NET, which I maintain at [Synadia](https://www.synadia.com/).
* [Cienvita on GitHub](https://github.com/cienvita), the open source
  projects we run at Cienvita, including
  [msgsrvd](https://github.com/cienvita/msgsrvd), a durable message log
  written in C.
* [dotnet/runtime](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/pull/135783), a
  padding option for `Base64Url` (in review).
* [Cheat!](https://mtmk.github.io/cheat/), a Blazor WebAssembly app for
  cheating at word games. It loads an entire English word list into the
  browser, which is more than a web page should, and it works.
* [logz](https://github.com/mtmk/logz), a TCP log sink with single-file
  clients for debugging several processes at once.
* [ProtobufSockets](https://github.com/mtmk/ProtobufSockets), a
  publish/subscribe socket wrapper for .NET using protobuf-net, from
  2014.

More, mostly experiments, on [GitHub](https://github.com/mtmk).

## Why mtmk

When I opened my GitHub account, "ziya" was already taken. I wanted
something short and unique, so I did what novelists do and picked a pen
name: Maxwell Troy Milton King, chosen because searching for it returned
nothing at all. The initials, mtmk, have been my username ever since, or
_mtmk where even that was taken, as on X.
